Permits for concrete, driveways and walls in Frederick

Who issues building permits in Frederick?

Town of Frederick Building Department. Permit applications go through the Accela Citizen Access portal.

Source: frederickco.gov

What permit does driveway approach, curb or sidewalk work need in Frederick?

The Engineering Department requires a right-of-way permit for any work in the Town right-of-way, with a $150 non-refundable license fee plus a refundable deposit based on the amount of roadway cut. Applications are emailed and need a drawing of the work, a traffic control plan signed by an ATSSA-certified person and a work schedule, and Engineering schedules all inspections.

Source: frederickco.gov

Water, sod and sprinkler rules in Frederick

Watering rules change with drought stages and seasons. These are the rules the water provider published as of October 13, 2026; check the linked page before you seed, sod or change a sprinkler schedule.

Who supplies water in Frederick?

Town of Frederick east of I-25; Left Hand Water District west of I-25. Three suppliers are involved in potable water within town limits: the Town, Central Weld County Water District and Left Hand Water District. All residents east of I-25 are Town customers, and residents west of I-25 are Left Hand Water District customers.

Source: frederickco.gov

What are the outdoor watering rules in Frederick?

On May 20, 2026 the Town declared a Stage 1 (Mild) Water Shortage for its customers east of I-25. It recommends no watering between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m. and no more than three watering days per week, with voluntary compliance and a 10% reduction goal. Left Hand Water District, which serves Frederick west of I-25, has also declared a voluntary Stage 1.

Source: frederickco.gov

Can new sod or seed get extra water in Frederick?

The Town's 2026 Water Shortage Contingency Plan sets new-lawn (sod and seed) exceptions by permit. Stage 1 has no restrictions to exempt. In Stage 2, new lawns can be watered any day and time for the permitted period; in Stage 3 this applies only to September-to-April installations; Stages 4 and 5 allow no exceptions.

Source: frederickco.gov

Does a sprinkler backflow preventer need testing in Frederick?

Irrigation, commercial, industrial and fire lines must have an approved backflow assembly, tested by a certified tester at installation and every year after. Results go to the Town and Aqua Backflow, and double-check assemblies are not allowed on irrigation systems. For single-family homes, requirements vary by hazard, and the Town encourages testing irrigation systems every three years.

Source: frederickco.gov

Are there lawn replacement or sprinkler rebates in Frederick?

  • Lawn Replacement Rebate Program — Pays $2 per square foot, up to $2,000 per water account per year, for replacing at least 200 sq ft of lawn with low-water plantings or grasses; artificial turf is not eligible. Designs must be approved before work starts. 2026 projects had to be submitted Feb. 1 to Sept. 1 and completed and inspected by Oct. 20, 2026; open to Town water customers only. frederickco.gov
  • Irrigation Controller Rebate — Up to $200 utility-account credit for a new EPA WaterSense-certified Wi-Fi irrigation controller bought after Jan. 1, 2026, limited to one per household per 10 years. Open to Town water customers only; Left Hand Water District customers are referred to that district's rebates. frederickco.gov
  • Garden In A Box — Town of Frederick water customers get a discount on Resource Central's waterwise perennial garden kits. frederickco.gov
  • Slow the Flow — Free on-site sprinkler evaluation (about 75 minutes) and customized watering schedule from Resource Central for Town water customers. frederickco.gov

Also worth knowing in Frederick

  • Since Nov. 16, 2024 the Land Use Code no longer requires turf in front yards. Front-yard landscaped areas must have at least 50% living plant cover rather than all rock or mulch, and existing properties do not have to change their landscaping. frederickco.gov
  • Colorado 811 must be contacted at least three business days before any digging, fencing or construction, even on private property. Locates include oil and gas flowlines and gathering lines. frederickco.gov
