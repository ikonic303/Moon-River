Permits for concrete, driveways and walls in Louisville

Who issues building permits in Louisville?

City of Louisville Building Safety (Department of Planning and Building Safety).

Source: louisvilleco.gov

What permit does driveway approach, curb or sidewalk work need in Louisville?

Construction or utility work in a City street, sidewalk or easement needs a right-of-way or easement permit from Public Works before work starts. The fee is a $75 base plus $0.30 per linear foot of curb, gutter, sidewalk or driveway ramp, and all fees double if work starts first. Subcontractors must be licensed with the City, disturbed sidewalks and landscaping must be restored, and work carries a 2-year warranty; LMC 12.08.010 separately requires a permit to build or rebuild any sidewalk, curb or gutter.

Source: louisvilleco.gov · library.municode.com

Does a patio or driveway on private property need a building permit in Louisville?

The City's permit table says pouring an uncovered concrete patio needs no permit. Driveway repair and sidewalk repair may need one; contact Public Works first.

Source: louisvilleco.gov

When does a retaining wall need a permit in Louisville?

A retaining wall under 48 inches from the bottom of the footing to the top of the wall, with no surcharge, doesn't need a building permit. Walls over 48 inches, and any wall with a surcharge, need stamped engineered plans, and the City's permit table says to check with the Planning Department first (Minor Permit: Retaining Wall).

Source: louisvilleco.gov

Water, sod and sprinkler rules in Louisville

Watering rules change with drought stages and seasons. These are the rules the water provider published as of October 13, 2026; check the linked page before you seed, sod or change a sprinkler schedule.

Who supplies water in Louisville?

City of Louisville Public Works - Water and Sewer. Drinking water comes from South Boulder Creek and the Colorado and Fraser Rivers and is treated at two City plants.

Source: louisvilleco.gov

What are the outdoor watering rules in Louisville?

Louisville has no mandatory watering restrictions in 2026 and is asking for voluntary conservation. The City asked residents to keep sprinklers off until mid-to-late May, keep water off pavement, and end lawn watering by October 1; hand watering of trees, shrubs and gardens can continue.

Source: louisvilleco.gov · content.govdelivery.com

Can new sod or seed get extra water in Louisville?

For 2026 the City strongly recommended that residents hold off on establishing a new lawn and consider drought-tolerant landscaping instead.

Source: content.govdelivery.com

Does a sprinkler backflow preventer need testing in Louisville?

City design criteria require a backflow preventer on residential irrigation systems, and installing one needs a minor plumbing permit. City code (LMC 13.08.180) requires backflow assemblies on identified hazardous cross-connections to be tested at installation and every year after, at the customer's cost; the City uses Backflow Solutions, Inc. (BSI) to track assemblies.

Source: louisvilleco.gov · library.municode.com

Are there lawn replacement or sprinkler rebates in Louisville?

  • Garden In A Box (Resource Central, in partnership with the City) — Low-water garden kits offered through Resource Central in partnership with the City. The City lists no discount amount. content.govdelivery.com
  • Slow the Flow sprinkler evaluation (Resource Central) — Free sprinkler evaluations offered with the City. content.govdelivery.com
  • Lawn replacement incentives (Resource Central) — Incentives for replacing lawn with water-wise landscaping. The City lists no dollar amount. louisvilleco.gov

Also worth knowing in Louisville

  • Before digging for right-of-way work, call Colorado 811 (1-800-922-1987) for utility locates. louisvilleco.gov
