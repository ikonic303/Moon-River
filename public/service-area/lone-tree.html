Working in Lone Tree

Lone Tree is home to about 14,253 people, and Lone Tree sits in Douglas County. Lone Tree sits against the first rise of the foothills at about 6,000 feet, where slopes shed water toward whatever is downhill of them. That is a grading and retaining question before it is a concrete question: a patio or driveway here has to move water somewhere deliberate, and a wall holding a cut bank has to be built to stay put through a wet spring.

We are a Brighton contractor, and Lone Tree is part of the area we cover from there. Whichever job brings you to us, the same thing is true of all of it: somebody comes out and looks at the actual site before a number exists, because ground, drainage and what is already buried decide most of what a job costs and none of that survives a phone description.

Driveways, sidewalks, patios and slabs, finished broom or stamped. Replacement of concrete that has cracked, heaved or settled past repair, and honest advice on which of those two you are actually looking at.

How long does a quote take?

We come out and look at it. Concrete, grading and irrigation all depend on things that do not survive a phone description - how the ground sits, where water goes, what is already in the ground. The quote is free and there is no obligation attached to it.

When is the right time of year for this in Lone Tree?

At about 6,000 feet, the season is shorter on both ends than it is down on the metro floor. Concrete and sod both have narrower windows here, and irrigation needs to be winterized earlier. Interior work - remodeling, flooring, painting - runs year round, and winter is usually the easiest time to get it scheduled.
