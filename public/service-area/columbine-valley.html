Permits for concrete, driveways and walls in Columbine Valley

Who issues building permits in Columbine Valley?

Town of Columbine Valley Building Department. The Town issues residential building permits from Town Hall, 2 Middlefield Rd. (303-795-1434), with applications filed through its GovPilot portal and inspections by contracted inspectors. Every contractor and subcontractor on a permit must hold a Town contractor license, and exterior changes need HOA approval.

Source: columbinevalley.org

What permit does driveway approach, curb or sidewalk work need in Columbine Valley?

Work in the Town right-of-way, and any permanent private use of it, requires a Street Cut and Right of Way Permit under the Town's Rights of Way ordinance (Code Ch. 12.13), applied for through GovPilot.

Source: columbinevalley.org

Water, sod and sprinkler rules in Columbine Valley

Watering rules change with drought stages and seasons. These are the rules the water provider published as of October 13, 2026; check the linked page before you seed, sod or change a sprinkler schedule.

Who supplies water in Columbine Valley?

Denver Water. The Town lists Denver Water as the water provider (general 303-893-2444) and Platte Canyon Water & Sanitation as the sewer provider.

Source: denverwater.org · columbinevalley.org

What are the outdoor watering rules in Columbine Valley?

Under Denver Water's Stage 1 drought (declared Mar. 25, 2026), lawns could be watered on two assigned days a week (single-family even addresses Sun/Thu, odd Wed/Sat; multifamily, commercial, HOA and government Tue/Fri) and never from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Since Oct. 1, 2026 all lawn watering and spray irrigation are prohibited until further Board action (trees, shrubs and perennials may be hand-watered with a shutoff nozzle or drip-irrigated any day), with single-family fines of $500, then $1,000.

Source: denverwater.org

Can new sod or seed get extra water in Columbine Valley?

Denver Water's watering establishment permit allowed extra watering for new seed or sod (up to 3 weeks; 8 weeks for native grass seed), but as of Oct. 1, 2026 it is issued only for Lead Replacement Program, mainline pipe replacement or Certificate of Occupancy projects. Other grass establishment after Sept. 30, 2026 requires an approved variance.

Source: denverwater.org

Does a sprinkler backflow preventer need testing in Columbine Valley?

Denver Water requires backflow assemblies, including those on irrigation services, to be tested by a certified tester at installation and annually, with irrigation assemblies tested when the water is turned on for the May–September season. Reports go to crossconnectioncontrol@denverwater.org, and a $250 penalty applies if three mailed notices go unanswered and the account enters suspension.

Source: denverwater.org

Are there lawn replacement or sprinkler rebates in Columbine Valley?

  • Denver Water outdoor (rotary nozzle) rebate — Up to $3 per rotary or high-efficiency sprinkler nozzle (Hunter MP, Rain Bird, Toro Precision, K-Rain) for 10 to 100 nozzles, once per residence per 10 years, while funds last. denverwater.org
  • Turfgrass removal discount (Denver Water with Resource Central) — An upfront discount of up to $750 on turf removal services; Denver Water says all 2026 discounts have been allocated. denverwater.org

Also worth knowing in Columbine Valley

  • The Town right-of-way generally extends about 3 feet past the edge of the gutter pan; residents may landscape it but it stays public property, plantings must not block sightlines, and a 3-foot clear radius must be kept around fire hydrants. columbinevalley.org
  • The Town and utilities are not responsible for damage to irrigation lines, invisible dog fences or other private features in the right-of-way during maintenance, utility work or snow removal. columbinevalley.org
