Working in Brighton

Brighton is a city of roughly 40,083, and Brighton straddles Adams and Weld counties. Brighton sits on the South Platte River at the north-east edge of the Denver metro, at about 4,990 feet. There is open ground on most sides and very little natural shade or windbreak: lawns dry out from the edges in, irrigation coverage matters more than irrigation volume, and a patio or driveway takes sun and wind from every direction.

Permits for concrete, driveways and walls in Brighton

Who issues building permits in Brighton?

City of Brighton Community Development – Building Division (One Stop permit counter). Permits and contractor licenses are applied for through the City's OpenGov portal; the One Stop counter (1stop@brightonco.gov, 303-655-2017) also handles right-of-way and utility permits.

Source: brightonco.gov

What permit does driveway approach, curb or sidewalk work need in Brighton?

Concrete work in the street right-of-way (such as curb, gutter, sidewalk or a driveway approach) needs a Right-of-Way permit through the One Stop permit counter before work starts, and the contractor must hold a City of Brighton contractor's license. The permit requires a traffic control plan, a site plan and a bond (at least $5,000 for repair/replace work, or 115% of job value above $4,348), and the contractor must give 24 hours' notice for inspection before concrete is placed.

Source: brightonco.gov

When does a retaining wall need a permit in Brighton?

Brighton's 'Do I Need a Building Permit?' handout (2021 IRC) says retaining walls not over 4 feet tall, measured from the bottom of the footing to the top of the wall, do not need a building permit. Taller walls are not on the exempt list.

Source: brightonco.gov

Water, sod and sprinkler rules in Brighton

Watering rules change with drought stages and seasons. These are the rules the water provider published as of October 13, 2026; check the linked page before you seed, sod or change a sprinkler schedule.

Who supplies water in Brighton?

City of Brighton Utilities Department (Water).

Source: brightonco.gov

What are the outdoor watering rules in Brighton?

Stage 1 drought restrictions (approved April 7, 2026, extended through Nov. 15, 2026) limit irrigation to two days a week: even addresses Tuesday and Friday, odd addresses Wednesday and Saturday, and unassigned HOA/landscape tracts Monday and Friday. Irrigation is banned 10 a.m.–6 p.m., water may not pool in gutters or streets, and hand watering with a hose held in the hand or a watering can is allowed.

Source: brightonco.gov

Can new sod or seed get extra water in Brighton?

Through Sept. 30, 2026, installing or replacing sod on areas over 200 sq ft was banned; starting Oct. 1, 2026, the City issues free sod permits (apply through OpenGov). Permit schedule: up to 3 waterings a day in weeks 1–2, twice a day in weeks 3–4 and once a day in weeks 5–6, with no watering 10 a.m.–6 p.m. after week 2, each watering 10 minutes or less, and normal restrictions after six weeks.

Source: brightonco.gov

Does a sprinkler backflow preventer need testing in Brighton?

Residential irrigation systems must be protected from backflow, either isolated from household plumbing or with a containment assembly if on a separate service line, and containment assemblies must be tested annually with reports sent to the City. Testers must send passing reports to Brighton Utilities (backflow@brightonco.gov) within 10 business days and report failed tests within 24 hours.

Source: brightonco.gov

Are there lawn replacement or sprinkler rebates in Brighton?

  • Lawn Removal Incentive — Residential customers get $1 per sq ft of turf removed, up to $1,000. The design must be approved before work starts, have 70% native plant coverage and include no artificial turf; new builds and first-time landscaping are not eligible. brightonco.gov
  • WaterSense Smart Irrigation Controller Rebate — Rebate of 30% of the cost of an eligible WaterSense smart (ET) irrigation controller, one per customer. brightonco.gov
  • Garden In A Box (Resource Central) — Brighton Utilities customers can get a limited number of $25 discounts on Resource Central xeric garden kits; 2026 sales opened March 11. brightonco.gov

Also worth knowing in Brighton

  • Repairing the sidewalk, curb and gutter is the abutting property owner's responsibility (Municipal Code 12-8-20), and replacements must meet City standards and be done by a licensed, insured and bonded contractor. The City's 50/50 program pays half of sidewalk, curb and gutter replacement while funds last, but does not cover driveways or concrete on private property. brightonco.gov
