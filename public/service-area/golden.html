Working in Golden

Golden is home to about 20,399 people, and Golden sits in Jefferson County. Golden sits at the base of the foothills on the west edge of the metro, at about 5,670 feet. On sloped lots, water heads for whatever is downhill, which makes it a grading and retaining question before it is a concrete question: a patio or driveway has to move water somewhere deliberate, and a wall holding a cut bank has to stay put through a wet spring.

Below is what Golden's own offices and water provider publish about permits, watering and backflow testing for the kind of work we do, checked October 13, 2026, with a link to each source.

Permits for concrete, driveways and walls in Golden

Who issues building permits in Golden?

City of Golden Building Division. Contact: (303) 384-8151, building@cityofgolden.net.

Source: cityofgolden.gov

What permit does driveway approach, curb or sidewalk work need in Golden?

All work in Golden's right-of-way requires a Right of Way permit from Public Works, applied for through the City's online portal; contractors must be registered with the City and show general liability insurance. Concrete pours must be inspected by a Public Works inspector scheduled at least one business day ahead, and a traffic control plan is required for any occupancy of streets, sidewalks, trails or alleys.

Source: cityofgolden.gov

Does a patio or driveway on private property need a building permit in Golden?

Golden's Required Building Permits Guide lists patio and porch covers as needing a permit and landscaping as not needing one; it does not address on-grade patios, driveways or other flatwork and directs residents to the Building Division for anything unclear.

Source: cityofgolden.gov

When does a retaining wall need a permit in Golden?

Golden requires a permit, with engineering, for retaining or landscape walls (individually or in combination) over 4 feet tall measured from the bottom of the footing. Walls 4 feet or less are exempt unless they support a surcharge or are on a corner lot or in a historic district.

Source: cityofgolden.gov

Water, sod and sprinkler rules in Golden

Watering rules change with drought stages and seasons. These are the rules the water provider published as of October 13, 2026; check the linked page before you seed, sod or change a sprinkler schedule.

Who supplies water in Golden?

City of Golden Utilities Division.

Source: cityofgolden.gov

What are the outdoor watering rules in Golden?

Golden has been in Stage 1 drought since May 1, 2026; its Amended Stage 1 Drought Order (effective July 19, 2026, in force until Oct. 31, 2026 unless rescinded earlier) limits lawns, trees, shrubs and perennials to two irrigation events per Sunday-Saturday week per property, on days the customer chooses, between 6 p.m. and 10 a.m., and bans all outdoor irrigation, including hand and drip watering, from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Potted plants and food gardens may be hand-watered any day within the 6 p.m.-10 a.m. window, and power washing hardscapes and hand-washing vehicles at home are prohibited.

Source: cityofgolden.gov

Can new sod or seed get extra water in Golden?

Golden's Amended Stage 1 order grants no exceptions for new sod, turfgrass installation, native plantings or turf establishment watering; new plantings stay on the two-events-per-week limit and the 6 p.m.-10 a.m. window. The temporary irrigation exemptions Golden normally offers for new seed, sod, native gardens, trees and shrubs are not available during declared drought stages.

Source: cityofgolden.gov

Does a sprinkler backflow preventer need testing in Golden?

Golden requires backflow prevention on irrigation connections (among other connections), and assemblies must be tested annually by a certified backflow tester with reports emailed to backflow@cityofgolden.net; failing to test can lead to termination of service.

Source: cityofgolden.gov

Are there lawn replacement or sprinkler rebates in Golden?

  • Smart irrigation controller rebate — Residential: 50% of the pre-tax price of a qualifying smart controller, up to $200; applicants must be registered on AquaHawk, new construction is not eligible, and funding is first-come, first-served. cityofgolden.gov
  • Rotary/high-efficiency nozzle rebate — $4 per nozzle when replacing fixed spray nozzles with accepted rotary models, up to $200 for residential customers registered on AquaHawk. cityofgolden.gov
  • Drip irrigation equipment rebate — Up to 50% of drip equipment costs, capped at $200 per household, for converting overhead spray irrigation to drip; new construction does not qualify. cityofgolden.gov
  • Rain sensor rebate — Full pre-tax price of a qualifying rain sensor added to an existing controller, up to $50 for residential customers. cityofgolden.gov
  • Lawn Replacement and Garden In A Box (Resource Central) — Golden's water conservation page points residents to Resource Central's Lawn Replacement and Garden In A Box programs; no amounts are listed. cityofgolden.gov

Also worth knowing in Golden

  • Connecting a lawn sprinkler system to the water supply (backflow) requires a Golden building permit, and fences over 7 feet tall or in a front yard, on a corner lot or in a historic district need a permit. cityofgolden.gov
  • Golden's drought order requires leaks to be repaired within ten calendar days of City notice and bars irrigation water from pooling or running off into streets, sidewalks, gutters or storm drains. cityofgolden.gov
